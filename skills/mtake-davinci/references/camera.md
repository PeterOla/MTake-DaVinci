# Camera, locations and spatial continuity

The movement sheet is a plain overhead drawing a director can read without opening the renderer. Place the actual road, window, doorway, route or work surface and identify the object people track. Draw an arrow along the camera's path. Add the camera height and a facing arrow or sight cone at the start, at changes of direction or height, and at the stop. Label the times and the start and end compositions. A sideways glance and a move across town look different on this sheet.

Use the same sheet throughout one scene. Mark a cut with a break and identify the landmark that survives it. For a genuine new scene, agree its move before detailed art. Existing approval carries to later beats on the same path. A reroute that changes what the viewer can see goes back into the recorded decision. Record delegation when the director has given it. **How to earn a reveal.** Start below a roofline with the street visible on both sides. As the camera rises, the nearest cornice passes first; a real gap between roofs reveals the distant landmark. Keep the landmark in view when turning. If the point is the neighbourhood, end where several houses and the space between them still read. An attractive close-up can hide the very evidence the story needs. In a tabletop shot, the equivalent landmarks are the table edge, the row and the empty interval at its end.

**From place to map.** Start at a recognisable field, street or shoreline. Rise continuously until a second landmark and the route between them can be seen. Align the map's rotation and scale to that view; fade in boundaries and then names as they become useful. Give the viewer time to follow the distance before speaking its value. If returning to the origin, push into the same mark until its real street geometry takes over. In a country map use licensed boundary data, fine strokes, restrained relief and an unobstructed label hierarchy. Keep the data source, date, projection and any disputed-boundary treatment with the scene. Source paper grain or generate it procedurally.

**Build the set once.** Place model origins, ground level and one real dimension in scene units. Store camera keys and set coordinates. Build roads, kerbs, lot boundaries, roofs, rear doors, walks and adjoining blocks wherever the sheet exposes them. Reuse repeated lamps or windows as instances. Vary them where a site photo supports a difference. Ensure a seated person's feet, thighs, back, hands and the chair have plausible contact from every camera angle. Check the return angle and changed state in the same world.

For a phone review, export the first pose, the moment of greatest overlap and the final reveal from the actual camera. Then play the move at 1×. A transition that works as three stills can still hesitate at the window, expose an empty set, reverse direction or conceal the clue.
